RLVR with GRPO:
sample, verify, reinforce.

A written companion to the visual tutorial, following the path from a checkable answer to a group-relative policy update.

Watch on YouTube ↗ · Numerical examples in the film are illustrative, not measured experiment results.

01 / Start with a checkable outcome

In reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), a language model samples a completion for a prompt. A verifier checks the outcome: for example, whether the final answer matches a known solution. That check supplies the reward. The verifier’s reliability and coverage determine what the reward can actually tell us.

Supervised fine-tuning learns from supplied demonstrations. RLVR instead optimizes rewards on sampled completions, including the model’s own mistakes. These are complementary training tools; the tutorial describes a supervised warm start followed by reinforcement learning.

02 / Let the group supply the baseline

Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) samples several completions for the same prompt and compares their rewards. Rather than training a separate value model, it uses the group’s reward statistics to construct an advantage for each completion. See DeepSeekMath, which introduced GRPO.

Aᵢ = (rᵢ − mean(r)) / std(r)

In the film’s example, three of eight completions succeed. With binary rewards, the mean is 0.375 and the population standard deviation is approximately 0.484. Successful completions receive an advantage of about +1.29; unsuccessful ones receive about −0.77.

If every completion receives the same reward, there is no relative learning signal. Implementations must handle the zero standard deviation safely. This means no reward-driven policy-gradient signal from that group; an independently applied regularization term can still contribute.

03 / Turn outcome feedback into token updates

An outcome reward scores a whole trajectory. Its advantage is shared across the generated tokens, rather than identifying which individual reasoning step was responsible for success. This is a practical learning signal, but a coarse form of credit assignment.

The GRPO objective compares current token probabilities with those under the old policy. A PPO-style clipped objective limits the incentive to move the probability ratio too far on a batch. A reference-policy KL penalty can also be included. Normalization choices and the use of that penalty vary across training recipes. Understanding R1-Zero-Like Training examines normalization biases and introduces Dr. GRPO.

04 / Mask what the model did not generate

With tools, a trajectory alternates between model-generated tokens and environment responses. Tool outputs remain part of the context, but are masked out of the policy loss: the model did not sample those tokens. The reward can then check whether the task was solved and whether the tool calls met the relevant requirements.

05 / Separate generation from training

Sampling multiple long completions adds substantial work. In a synchronous pipeline, a slow trajectory can hold up an entire batch. Asynchronous generation and training overlap these jobs, but introduce a new complication: the policy that generated a sample can lag behind the trainer.

The tutorial separates two probability ratios. The current-to-old-policy ratio controls the policy update. The old-to-behavior-policy ratio accounts for the distribution that actually generated the sample. Truncating the latter can reduce extreme weights, with a corresponding bias–variance tradeoff. AReaL develops this decoupled asynchronous training approach.

πθ / μ = (πθ / πold) × (πold / μ)

Sample. Verify. Reinforce.

Sample a group, verify the outcomes, and compare rewards within the group. Apply the learning signal to generated tokens, constrain the policy update, and account for the sampling policy when generation and training are decoupled.
