Pessimism pays off:
off-policy learning, from recommendation to LLM reasoning.

A written companion to the visual tutorial: a review of work by the Recommendation Research Group at Criteo AI Lab and collaborators, on why learning from someone else’s data should be pessimistic rather than merely corrected.

Watch on YouTube ↗ · Results quoted in the film are those reported in the cited papers; curves marked illustrative are schematic.

01 / Two worlds, one problem

A recommender learns from logs. A logging policy μ chose which item to show, and we observe the reward only for that item, never for the alternatives. Large-scale reinforcement learning for language models increasingly looks the same. Rollouts are produced by an older policy, or by a separate inference engine, while the trainer has already moved on. In both cases the data comes from μ, and we want to improve a different policy πθ. That is off-policy learning, and the recurring question is how much to trust data someone else collected.

02 / Importance weights, and why they break

The classic correction is inverse propensity scoring (IPS):

V̂_IPS(π) = (1/n) Σ_i [ π(a_i | x_i) / μ(a_i | x_i) ] · r_i

It is unbiased in expectation, but when the logging policy rarely chose an action the new policy favours, single samples receive enormous weights, and the estimate, and any optimizer following it, is dominated by a few of them.

03 / REINFORCE, PPO and GRPO under stale data

REINFORCE pushes up the log-probability of each sampled response, weighted by its advantage: ∇J = Ey∼πθ[Â ∇ log πθ(y|x)]. It is unbiased and on-policy. On stale data it needs the sequence-level ratio πθ/μ, a product of per-token ratios. A thousand factors each slightly above one produce an astronomical weight: IPS again, only worse. Lilian Weng’s Policy Gradient Algorithms is a good background on off-policy policy gradients and importance sampling.

PPO uses per-token ratios, clips them to 1 ± ε, and takes the minimum of the clipped and unclipped objectives. The PPO paper itself describes this minimum as a pessimistic lower bound on the unclipped objective. Pessimism is already present, as a heuristic trust region, while training is treated as nearly on-policy with a learned critic.

GRPO replaces the critic with a group baseline, but remains built on importance ratios. With asynchronous generation it adds a truncated behaviour-correction ratio (see the RLVR with GRPO companion). Corrections accumulate: ratios, clipping, truncation, KL penalties. The 2026 paper notes that such corrections can add variance, destabilize optimization and accelerate entropy collapse.

04 / The principle of pessimism

Estimates of a policy’s value are uncertain, and some policies look good only because a few samples received large weights. An optimist picks the best point estimate. A pessimist picks the policy with the best worst case, the highest lower confidence bound, preferring what the data can actually support.

05 / 2020: log-IPS and the Dual Bandit

Joint Policy-Value Learning for Recommendation (Jeunen, Rohde, Vasile, Bompaire; KDD 2020) ran the first broad empirical study of counterfactual learning for recommendation in simulation. It found that standard IPS struggles with sparse, stochastic rewards such as clicks. Its logarithmic variant reweights the log-probability instead of the probability, which convexifies the objective:

V̂_log(π) = (1/n) Σ_i [ r_i / μ(a_i | x_i) ] · log π(a_i | x_i)

This is a reward-weighted log-likelihood, as the released code makes explicit. The Dual Bandit combines this policy objective with a value model’s likelihood under shared parameters, joining policy learning and value learning.

06 / 2024: logarithmic smoothing

Logarithmic Smoothing for Pessimistic Off-Policy Evaluation, Selection and Learning (Sakhi, Aouali, Alquier, Chopin) makes pessimism the principle. It derives fully empirical, high-probability bounds for a broad class of importance-weighting estimators, and seeks the tightest one. That leads to the LS estimator, with costs c ∈ [−1, 0]:

R̂_LS^λ(π) = −(1/n) Σ_i (1/λ) · log(1 − λ w_i c_i)

Small weights pass almost unchanged, while large weights are compressed logarithmically: a smooth, differentiable form of clipping. Expanded, it penalizes all moments of the weights rather than only the variance. Its bound is provably tighter than competing ones, which translates into better policy selection and learning.

07 / Drop the weights

Return to off-policy REINFORCE and remove the importance weight. What remains is a weighted log-likelihood on the logged data, Ey∼μ[Â · log πθ(y|x)], the same shape as log-IPS. With non-negative weights g(r), its solution is π* ∝ μ · g(r). It can only place probability where the data already has some, a built-in form of pessimism. Its weak spot is negative advantages, which push log-probabilities toward −∞.

08 / 2025: RoiRL

RoiRL (Arzhantsev, Sakhi, Vasile) targets self-supervised reasoning. Test-time RL (TTRL) uses majority-vote rewards but relies on heavy online RL. RoiRL instead runs offline iterations of weighted log-likelihood: sample answers, weight them by the majority-vote reward, fine-tune, repeat.

θ_m = argmax_θ E_{y∼π_{m−1}}[ g(r̃) · log π_θ(y|x) ] ⇒ π_m ∝ π_0 · exp( (1/β) Σ_{j≤m} r̃_j )

Iterating reaches the same regularized target without a reference model and without importance weights. The paper reports training up to 2.5× faster than TTRL, with consistently better results on reasoning benchmarks.

09 / 2026: pessimism, made explicit

Off-Policy Learning to Reason Works Because It Is More Pessimistic Than You Think (Sakhi, Arzhantsev, Aouali, Vasile) studies weight-free off-policy objectives of the form

E_{y∼π_old}[ Â · log π_θ(y|x) − (β/2) · ( log π_θ(y|x) / π_old(y|x) )² ]

which cover recent methods such as A*-PO and OAPL. Their fixed point is not the usual exponential tilt but a Lambert-W-tempered target:

π*(y|x) = ( π_old(y|x) / τ ) · W₀( τ · e^{A(x,y)/β} )

Because W₀ grows roughly logarithmically, high advantages are tempered rather than amplified exponentially: the effective target is more conservative than the nominal objective suggests. This explains why OAPL’s decoupling of two temperatures stabilizes training. It also motivates a principled fix, the β-shifted mean advantage Âi = ri − r̄ + β, which places training in the pessimistic regime by construction. The paper reports stable training under weak regularization and very stale rollouts (Qwen3-8B, math benchmarks).

10 / Side by side

  • REINFORCE: on-policy; off-policy it needs sequence-level weights. Implicit target: the reward maximizer. No pessimism.
  • PPO: nearly on-policy; clipped per-token ratios and a critic. Implicit target (with KL): an exponential tilt of the reference. Heuristic pessimism through the min and clip.
  • GRPO (async): lagged data; clipped ratios plus truncated behaviour correction and a group baseline. Heuristic pessimism, stacked corrections.
  • Weight-free, pessimistic: off-policy by design; no importance weights; a centred or group advantage. The target is Lambert-tempered, so pessimism is built into the target itself.

Logged. Tempered. Trusted.

Logged recommendations and stale rollouts pose the same problem. Correcting harder with importance weights tends to trade bias for variance and instability; being pessimistic trades a little optimism for stability. Across this line of work the logarithm keeps reappearing as the practical tool: in log-IPS, in logarithmic smoothing, in weighted log-likelihood training, and in the Lambert-tempered targets of off-policy reasoning.
